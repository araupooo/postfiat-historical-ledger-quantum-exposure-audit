Headline finding

The ledger's permanently recorded history contains one signature-bearing record class in meaningful quantity: transactions. Validations, proposals, manifests and payment-channel claims are not recorded in ledgers at all — they are ephemeral. A future quantum adversary cannot harvest them from history. What history does record, permanently and publicly, is every transaction signature ever validated, with its public key. Forging a key lets an adversary submit new transactions as the account; it cannot rewrite validated history, which sits under the SHA-512Half hash chain.

Mitigation verdict

Full re-signing of historical transactions is not feasible: the private keys are held by users and mostly unavailable, and re-signing would cost ~7.58 GB of ML-DSA-65 signatures for the extrapolated full history while leaving the classical signatures in place. The recommended substitute is post-quantum checkpoints: one ML-DSA-65 signature (3,309 B) over each 1,000th ledger hash — about 26.5 MB for the whole history — plus key rotation and the live ML-DSA migration for new records. See mitigation_design.md for the phased plan, costs and open questions.

Limits. This is a sample of recent history, not the full history (see assumptions.md). Zero counts for validations, manifests, proposals and payment-channel claims are structural — those records are not in ledgers — not measurements of absence. The ledger was not compiled or executed.
